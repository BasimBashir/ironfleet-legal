# Supply and Logistics Services Agreement (Short Form)

Between: Southern Haulage Ltd ("the Supplier"), a fictional New Zealand freight and
warehousing company, and Harbour Freight Solutions Ltd ("the Customer"), a fictional
freight-forwarding company. This document is invented for end-to-end software testing;
no real parties, debts, or security interests exist.

## Term and services
The Supplier provides line-haul trucking, container devanning, and bonded warehousing
to the Customer at the Supplier's fictional Lyttelton depot, on continuing monthly
terms from 1 January 2019 until terminated by three months' written notice. Rates are
per the schedule issued each January; volumes fluctuate with the Customer's seasonal
import programme.

## Payment terms
The Customer pays each monthly statement by the 20th day of the following month. Any
amount not paid by its due date bears default interest at 26% per annum from due date
until payment, and the Supplier may, on seven days' written notice, suspend services
and withhold release of goods in its possession for any Customer in default, without
prejudice to its other rights. All amounts are exclusive of GST.

## Retention of title
Title to warehoused goods handled by the Supplier does not pass to the Customer's
sub-bailees except as arranged in writing; the Supplier's own equipment (pallets,
cages, strapping) remains the Supplier's property at all times, and the Customer must
return it on demand. The parties record their intention that the Supplier's interest
in its equipment and in unpaid-for services is to be secured under the Personal
Property Securities Act 1999 and registered on the Personal Property Securities
Register accordingly.

## Disputes
Any dispute about a statement must be notified in writing within 10 working days of
receipt, identifying the disputed amount and grounds; amounts not disputed within
that window are treated as accepted and payable. Nothing in this clause defers payment
of undisputed amounts. The parties will confer, then mediate, before either may
proceed in the Christchurch High Court. New Zealand law governs.
